# Genomic surveillance of Ebola virus

All this information is from István Albert and it is written in The Biostar Handbook (2nd Edition). 

The high mortality rate of the 2014 Ebola epidemic made it difficult for researchers to collect viral samples and study the pathogen’s evolution. Gire et al. analyzed Ebola epidemiology using 99 whole‑genome sequences obtained from 78 affected individuals. Their examination of sequence changes suggests that the outbreak likely originated from the spread of the virus from central Africa during the past decade. According to their findings, the epidemic began with a single transmission event from an unknown animal reservoir into the human population, after which two viral lineages from Guinea spread through person‑to‑person transmission into Sierra Leone. We intended to compare Ebola genomes from the 2014 and 2017 outbreaks, but because another outbreak occurred in 2026, the newly sequenced data can also be compared with the earlier genomes.
